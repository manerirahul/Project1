## 1. Demand is concentrated in two narrow windows

- The single busiest hour is **12:00** with **2,520 orders** across the year.
- Lunch (11:00-13:00) and dinner (17:00-20:00) together carry **68.3%** of all orders.
- **So what:** labour and oven capacity, not demand, is the binding constraint at midday.
- **Action:** roster to the hourly curve rather than flat shifts, and pre-prep the top A-class products before 11:00.

## 2. Weekly rhythm is stable and predictable

- Strongest average day: **Friday** ($2,721 per day). Weakest: **Sunday** ($1,908 per day).
- The gap between best and worst weekday is **43%**.
- **So what:** the variance is a weekday effect, not noise, so it is plannable.
- **Action:** move promotions to the two weakest weekdays; protect margin on peak days.

## 3. Revenue is flat across the year - growth has to come from mix

- Best month: **Jul** ($72,558). Weakest month: **Oct** ($64,028).
- Month-over-month growth stays within **9.9%** in either direction.
- Only **358 of 365 days** recorded any sale, so 7 days had zero revenue - worth confirming these were planned closures rather than missing data.
- **Action:** treat the baseline as mature; pursue basket size and product mix, not more footfall.

## 4. Product mix follows a clear Pareto pattern

- **21 of 32 products (A-class)** generate the first 80% of revenue.
- Top seller: **Thai Chicken** at $43,434 (5.31% of revenue).
- Weakest performer: **Brie Carre** at $11,588 (1.42%).
- **4 C-class products** together contribute only 6.9% while consuming menu space and stock.
- **Action:** run a menu-rationalisation review on C-class items; every delisting frees prep time at peak.

## 5. Category revenue is evenly balanced; size is where the money is

- Categories sit within **3.2 percentage points** of each other - led by **Classic** (26.91%).
- By contrast **L** alone drives **45.89%** of revenue.
- **So what:** customers are not category-loyal, but they are size-sensitive, and size is the highest-margin lever available.
- **Action:** make upsizing the default prompt at order entry; the price step between sizes is pure incremental margin on the same prep time.

## 6. Basket size is the biggest untapped lever

- **38.0%** of orders contain just one pizza.
- Average order value is **$38.31** at 2.32 pizzas per order.
- Pairs that co-occur far above chance are listed in `vw_product_affinity` (lift greater than 1 means a genuine affinity, not just two popular items).
- **Action:** drive the top affinity pairs as suggested add-ons at checkout; a single extra pizza on 10% of one-pizza orders is a mid-single-digit revenue gain.

## Caveats a reviewer should know

- The source has no cost, margin, store, channel or customer identifier, so profitability and retention are out of scope. Recommendations are framed on revenue and mix only.
- `is_vegetarian` is derived by matching meat terms in the recipe text; it is an analytical convenience, not a certified dietary claim.
- Affinity is computed at product level ignoring size, which is the correct grain for a cross-sell prompt.
